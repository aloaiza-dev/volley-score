# Project Guidance

## Design Context

### Users
VolleyScore Pro is a mobile-first volleyball scoreboard used by a mixed audience: referees, coaches, players, parents, and tournament staff. It is used in live match contexts where attention is split between the court and the device, so interactions must be fast, obvious, and reliable on small screens. The core job is to let users configure and track a volleyball match with minimal friction, high confidence, and quick readability at a glance.

### Brand Personality
The product should feel professional, effective, precise, energetic, and playful. The tone should communicate trust and operational clarity first, while still feeling distinctly sports-oriented and lively rather than sterile. Visual choices should support confidence under pressure and make scorekeeping feel polished instead of improvised.

### Aesthetic Direction
Favor a clean, high-contrast sports utility aesthetic with strong hierarchy, bold numerals, and clear team differentiation. Light mode can be the default direction if it improves legibility in bright gym environments, but both light and dark themes should feel intentional and fully designed. Take inspiration from well-designed score tracking and live match apps that prioritize clarity, speed, and glanceability. Avoid generic dashboard styling, cluttered settings screens, muddy contrast, or visuals that feel childish or novelty-driven.

### Design Principles
1. Optimize for live-match speed: every critical action should be reachable quickly, easy to understand, and forgiving on small touch screens.
2. Make state unmistakable: score, sets, timer, active context, and destructive actions should always be visually clear at a glance.
3. Design for mixed environments: support bright gyms, dim venues, and one-handed mobile use with strong contrast and large tap targets.
4. Use energy with restraint: keep the interface lively and sports-driven, but never let decoration compete with scoring clarity.
5. Exceed baseline accessibility: include reduced-motion support, color-blind-safe distinctions, robust contrast, screen-reader-friendly labels, and touch targets that feel generous on phones.
